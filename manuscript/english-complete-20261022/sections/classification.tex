\begin{proof}[Geometric exhaustiveness and host representatives]
Every tile contains at most one outer midpoint, so the union $C\cup F$
contains at most two distinct such points. If $F$ contains $O$, assign G5
before inspecting this count. Otherwise $\kappa=0,1,2$ gives the asserted
alternatives. When $\kappa=1$, membership in $C$ takes precedence, including
shared membership in $C\cap F$; only exclusive membership in $F$ enters
residual G2. For $\kappa=2$, the two midpoints are either adjacent or
opposite, and each belongs to a different non-corner tile.

A midpoint not contained in $C\cup F$ must have a corner host among the
same four chosen corner tiles. A target corner not adjacent to its side
is at distance $\sqrt5\,\mu>\sqrt2$ from that midpoint, and so cannot be
its host. Distinct midpoints cannot choose the same tile, by the
unit-square diameter bound. Thus choosing one available adjacent host
for each unsupplied midpoint automatically gives an injective host map;
multiple available hosts are not discarded.

For residual G2 place the $F$ midpoint on $\BOTTOM$ by a target symmetry.
The left, top and right midpoint hosts belong respectively to
$\{1,4\},\{3,4\},\{2,3\}$. Removing repeated host indices leaves precisely:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}cccl@{}}
\toprule
RIGHT host & TOP host & LEFT host & Representative\\\midrule
$2$ & $3$ & $4$ & near, $W=V_1$\\
$3$ & $4$ & $1$ & reflection of near\\
$2$ & $4$ & $1$ & far, $W=V_3$\\
$2$ & $3$ & $1$ & reflection of far\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Reflection in the vertical central axis fixes the bottom midpoint and
pairs the indicated rows. It need not preserve a chosen corner index;
indices are relabelled by the reflected target geometry.

For adjacent owned midpoints, put those of $C,F$ on LEFT and BOTTOM.
The two unsupplied hosts have
$(\TOP,\RIGHT)\in\{(4,3),(4,2),(3,2)\}$.
For opposite owned midpoints, put those of $C,F$ on LEFT and RIGHT.
The four choices of bottom and top hosts reduce under horizontal
reflection to the three representatives
$(\BOTTOM,\TOP)=(2,3),(1,4),(1,3)$.
These are host choices in actual coverings, not Boolean assumptions
about a newly invented layout.
\end{proof}
