\begin{lemma}[The closed central box]\label{lem:central-box}
Once G5 is excluded for every target of side greater than two, the chosen
central tile contains $[-(\mu-1),\mu-1]^2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $K=(-\mu+1,\mu-1)^2$. Every $p\in K$ is the centre of some
axis-aligned square with half-side strictly greater than one lying in
$T_\mu$. The same six tiles cover this inscribed square. Universal G5
exclusion says exactly one tile contains $p$.
Consequently the six intersections of the tiles with $K$ form a disjoint
finite relatively closed partition of $K$. Each member is also relatively
open, since its complement is a finite union of relatively closed members.
Connectedness of $K$ permits only one nonempty member. It is the member
containing $O$, namely $C\cap K$. Thus $K\subset C$ and closedness gives
$\overline K\subset C$.

The use of an inscribed square permits its corner hosts to be chosen afresh.
This argument does not assume the later centre-no-mid theorem or root cap.
\end{proof}
